make.fun competition best-flight-game-20261126-0057 ($500, top 50 share; deadline 28 Nov 07:57 WIB). Build the best flight game: a complete, original, playable flight game in static files under game/, keyboard + mouse, no network, with screenshots showing a cockpit or chase view over recognisable terrain, the aircraft in frame with instruments lit, a runway or a target ring ahead, and altitude, speed and heading on the HUD.

Game: "Merapi Run" — fly a small propeller plane around a volcanic island. Chase view over a low-poly island (green hills, a snow-capped volcano, open sea, a runway on the south shore). Thread 8 orange rings, mind fuel, land gently on the runway to refuel (+150). Crash anywhere else or ditch in the sea and the flight ends. HUD: altitude (ft), speed (kt), heading (compass), fuel bar, score, rings x/8, an attitude ball, a throttle bar, a flight-path marker, and short messages. Controls: arrows/WASD pitch and bank, Shift/Ctrl throttle, mouse drag to bank, Space/click to start, P to pause. Screens: title, play, paused, crashed, mission complete. ?shot=start|play|end&seed=N stages a screenshot scene and sets window.__shotReady.

Files (one clear job each): game/index.html (canvas page), game/src/main.js (boot + fixed-step loop + screens), game/src/world.js (flight physics, terrain height, rings, fuel, landings, crashes), game/src/render.js (chase-view painter: sky, terrain grid, runway, rings, 3D aeroplane, HUD), game/src/input.js (keyboard + mouse), game/src/config.js (tuning numbers), game/src/random.js (seeded generator), game/src/shots.js (screenshot scenes). main.ts copies game/ + screenshots/ to /output and writes dossier.json with the vendored make.fun library. README.md documents how to fly and the file layout. Screenshots: 1-play.png (cover), 2-start.png, 3-end.png, all real renders of the game's own code in headless Chromium, 1280x800.

Judge criteria (most important first): a complete flight game in the screenshots (chase view, terrain, aircraft, instruments, runway/ring, HUD); fun and depth (throttle/turn discipline, rings + fuel + landing decisions); feel and polish (controls answer, camera never fights the pilot, landings and crashes read); look and originality (one terrain/sky style, a built-looking aircraft, flown instruments); structure (source split into files named for what they do). Never copy a commercial game's names, art or levels.
